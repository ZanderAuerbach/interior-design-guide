01

Katie Martinez Design

San Francisco · New England

KMD blends playful and refined — layering vintage, handcrafted, and custom-designed pieces with a sensitivity to color and pattern that feels collected rather than decorated. Her bathrooms use artisan tile, warm wood cabinetry, and natural stone to create spaces that feel both personal and timeless. The perfect balance of quiet and quirky.

Interior Design

Ripley Residence, Weston, MA  ·  Photography: Sian Richards

02

Mother Studio

Asheville, NC  ·  Working Nationally

Laura Evans and Karie Reinertson run a studio grounded in the culture of the handmade. Bespoke hand-painted tile, wood-paneled walls, and an easy interplay of pattern and natural material define bathrooms that feel rooted in craft and place. Nothing obvious. Nothing cheap. The Suppertime project was built in collaboration with Maryann Thompson Architects.

Interior Design + Craft

Suppertime, Martha's Vineyard  ·  Architecture: Maryann Thompson Architects  ·  Photography: Nicole Franzen

03

Reath Design

Los Angeles, CA  ·  Frances Merrill

Frances Merrill founded Reath Design in 2009 after working at Commune Design, and her studio is everything Commune is — but more layered, more personal. She uses wallpaper with abandon, mixes pattern and texture across eras, and brings an art-world sensibility that keeps rooms from tipping into decoration. Her bathrooms are known for a strong material gesture — graphic tile, a slab of book-matched marble, warm wood — held in tension with something unexpected: a vintage fixture, an odd color, a print that shouldn't work but does. Quiet confidence with a wink.

Interior Design

Portland Residence (powder room)  ·  Point Dume, Malibu  ·  Santa Monica Beach House  ·  Reath Design by Frances Merrill  ·  Via 1stDibs Introspective

04

Commune Design

Los Angeles, CA  ·  Roman Alonso & Steven Johanknecht

Commune is where Frances Merrill trained — a studio built on mid-century reference, material honesty, and an unfussy California sensibility. Their West Marin Retreat (above Tomales Bay) is a study in restraint with personality: limewashed walls, local craftwork, Edith Heath and Josef Frank tile in the bathrooms, and furniture by JB Blunk. Mid-century Scandinavian summer house meets Northern California landscape. Color and pattern turned up just enough.

Interior Design + Architecture

West Marin Retreat, Tomales Bay, CA  ·  Commune Design  ·  Bathrooms: Edith Heath + Josef Frank tile

05

Nickey Kehoe

Los Angeles · New York  ·  Todd Nickey & Amy Kehoe

Todd Nickey and Amy Kehoe run one of LA's most quietly distinctive studios — color-forward without being obvious, eclectic without being chaotic. Their interiors pull from textile history, vintage finds, and their own NK Collection to build rooms that feel genuinely inhabited. Bathrooms feature custom onyx or stone vanities, wallpaper murals (often Gracie Studio), and materials that age well: unlacquered brass, honed surfaces, richly grained wood. Bespoke and considered, never trend-driven.

Interior Design

Brentwood Residence & Santa Monica Canyon  ·  Nickey Kehoe  ·  Photography: D. Arnold

06

Studio KO

Paris · Marrakech · London  ·  Karl Fournier & Olivier Marty

Karl Fournier and Olivier Marty founded Studio KO in Paris in 1999 and have since become one of the most internationally admired practices of their generation — moving between rigorous Moroccan villas, London hospitality (Chiltern Firehouse), the Yves Saint Laurent Museum in Marrakech, boutiques for Aesop and Ami, and private residences across France, Morocco, Corsica, and Los Angeles. Their work holds a generative tension: "constantly torn between our desire for extremely pared-down, pure things and a more decorative, busy aesthetic." The common denominator is that their buildings are always completely in tune with their environment — materials, handicraft, landscape. Multiple features in World of Interiors.

Architecture · Interior Design · Paris · Marrakech

Luberon, France  ·  Villa K, Tagadert  ·  Villa E, Ourika  ·  Pierre Bergé Home, Tangier  ·  Studio KO  ·  Via 1stDibs Introspective

07

Arent&Pyke

Sydney & Melbourne, Australia  ·  Juliette Arent & Sarah-Jane Pyke

Juliette Arent and Sarah-Jane Pyke are among the most decorated interior designers in Australia and have become genuine international figures — regulars in World of Interiors, Vogue Living, and Elle Decoration, with a monograph published by Thames & Hudson. Their work is defined by an extraordinary layering of material, art, object, and memory: rooms that feel genuinely inhabited by specific people with specific pasts. Vintage finds and design classics appear alongside custom fabrication; color is used with restraint and confidence simultaneously. Their bathrooms are benchmark work — arched stone frames, freestanding baths, joinery floating above stone floors, custom marble islands. Nothing accidental; everything thought through in minute detail.

Interior Design · Custom Fabrication · Art Curation

Bradleys Head House, Mosman NSW  ·  Architecture: TKD Architects  ·  Photography: Anson Smart

Golden Light, Lower North Shore, Sydney  ·  Photography: Anson Smart  ·  Styling: Jack Milenkovic

08

Studioilse

London  ·  Ilse Crawford

Ilse Crawford is among the most original design thinkers of her generation — a studio that consistently asks what spaces are for, how they feel to inhabit, and what it means to be human within them. The work moves across scales and typologies: private homes, floating residences, rural coaching inns, Stockholm guest houses. What binds them is a commitment to sensory intelligence over visual novelty, and a deep literacy in materials, craft, and the domestic arts. She is equally at home with hand-stitched textiles and structural timber as with contemporary furniture or hand-painted antiques. Old and new in conversation, never in conflict.

Interior Design · Architecture · Strategy

Chalet, Switzerland

Switzerland  ·  2015  ·  Photography: Felix Odell

A family mountain home buried deep in the Swiss Alps. Working from a warm palette of natural materials — pitched timber, stone, hand-stitched textiles — Studioilse created a chalet that can adapt from festive gathering to quiet hibernation. A hand-painted botanical chest in the bedroom demonstrates the studio's belief in the singular object: one piece that anchors an entire room and earns its place through character, not price.

Chalet, Swiss Alps, 2015  ·  Photography: Felix Odell  ·  studioilse.com →

Shemara, Motor Yacht

Mallorca  ·  2015  ·  Photography: Casper Sejersen

The refurbishment of a historic motor yacht originally built in 1938. Studioilse's brief was to create a floating family home that made sense for both owners and crew — evoking the glamorous spirit of the boat's extraordinary history without becoming a slave to it. The result is a yacht interior that feels genuinely domestic: proper rooms, not cabins. The Shemara's story includes the Titanic; its interior should acknowledge history without pastiche.

Shemara, Motor Yacht, 2015  ·  Photography: Casper Sejersen  ·  studioilse.com →

Ett Hem, Number 4

Stockholm, Sweden  ·  2022  ·  Photography: Magnus Marding

A decade after Studioilse helped define a new language of hospitality with the original Ett Hem, they returned to expand the Stockholm guest house into the house next door. The same values, a new building: intimate and lively, domestic and public at once. Generous light from large windows opens onto rooftop views. Food and the local supply chain are foregrounded. The rooms are expansive but never grand — they feel like somewhere you might actually want to live.

Ett Hem, Number 4, Stockholm, 2022  ·  Photography: Magnus Marding  ·  studioilse.com →

The Olde Bell Inn

Hurley, Berkshire  ·  2007  ·  Photography: Lisa Cohen

A benchmark project: the contemporary reinterpretation of a traditional English coaching inn. Forty-seven bedrooms, a dining room, bar, malt house, and meadow garden — all reinterpreted using British materials, furniture, and local produce. The result is a rural hospitality model rooted in local life, not heritage pastiche. Old and new placed confidently side by side: exposed beams meet contemporary upholstery; antique mirrors hold company with clean-lined lighting. Craft, not costume.

The Olde Bell Inn, Hurley, Berkshire, 2007  ·  Photography: Lisa Cohen  ·  studioilse.com →

09

Rose Uniacke

London, Pimlico  ·  roseuniacke.com

Rose Uniacke is among the most consequential British interior designers of the last twenty years — a figure who has largely redefined what domestic elegance looks like in the contemporary era. Her signature is "sumptuous minimalism": period rooms approached with a pared-back choreography, where antiques from antiquity to the 20th century live alongside bespoke contemporary pieces and textiles. Light and proportion are paramount. Art — often by female artists working in textile, ceramics, and paper — is integral, not decorative afterthought. Her own fabric and paint lines extend the practice into a complete material ecosystem. Recent work includes London townhouses, a New York hotel (San Vicente West Village), the Belmond Royal Scotsman, and her personal Coach House — photographed by Francois Halard for World of Interiors.

Interior Design · Fabric · Paint · Antiques

San Vicente West Village  ·  New York  ·  Photography: Simon Upton

A Place with a Colorful History

A comprehensive interior renovation of a 1908 redbrick building in Manhattan's West Village — once part of the Jane Hotel, originally lodgings for visiting sailors. Rose Uniacke transformed it into a members' club and hotel for Jeff Klein: a quiet, layered sophistication grounded in the building's bones. As written in Architectural Digest: "Walking into a room by Rose feels like walking into the best version of the home you wish you owned."

Belmond Royal Scotsman  ·  Scotland

Edwardian Elegance Reimagined

A luxury sleeper train running through the Scottish Highlands, designed to evoke the comfort and pace of travel in a former era — without historical recreation. The cabins extend a theme of country house comfort: mahogany marquetry, tweeds from Holland & Sherry and Bute Mills, botanical watercolours by Mackintosh. Materials drawn from gorse and heather — purples, deep blues, greens, ambers. Familiar landscape encoded in fabric and finish.

Ben Pentreath

London & Dorset  ·  Ben Pentreath Ltd

Ben Pentreath is one of the most celebrated decorators working in Britain today — a classical architect and interior designer whose work draws equally from the great English practitioners of the 18th and 19th centuries and from the bold colour and pattern of the 1960s and 70s. His rooms combine an intuitive use of colour with classical architectural detail and a fresh modern sensibility: country houses, London townhouses, Dorset farmhouses, and Arts and Crafts manors, all treated with the same erudition and playful confidence. He writes extensively on architecture and decoration and is one of the most consistently cited designers in World of Interiors. "Just right for the client and the building" is the benchmark — nothing more, nothing less.

Grenney Greig

London & Tangier  ·  Veere Grenney & Timothy Greig

Veere Grenney — now Grenney Greig following his partnership with Timothy Greig — is among the most admired English decorators of his generation, a direct heir to John Fowler and the great country house tradition. His rooms have a perfected, deeply considered quality: the right pattern at the right scale, the right color in the right light. He has decorated houses in Tangier, London, Scotland, and New England; his Tangier work especially — bold Moroccan geometry alongside English antiques — shows a rare ability to bring two design cultures into genuine dialogue. Perennial World of Interiors presence.

Interior Decoration · Country Houses · London · Tangier

12

Studio Peregalli

Milan, Italy  ·  Roberto Peregalli & Laura Sartori Rimini

Roberto Peregalli and Laura Sartori Rimini are the most unapologetically theatrical decorators working today — and among the most brilliant. Their Milan studio creates rooms of extraordinary density and atmosphere: frescoed ceilings, hand-blocked wallpapers, antique textiles piled on antique furniture, lacquered surfaces, trompe-l'œil, boiserie, layers of objects and memory that add up to something far beyond decoration. They work for private clients in Milan, Rome, Paris, and New York; their houses have appeared in World of Interiors more times than almost any other studio. The aesthetic is distinctly Milanese — learned, slightly melancholic, fiercely opinionated — with roots in the great tradition of Italian interior culture that runs from Piero Fornasetti through Renzo Mongiardino.

Interior Design · Milan · Antiques · Fresco · Theatrical

Beata Heuman

London  ·  Beata Heuman Ltd

Beata Heuman — Swedish-born, London-based — trained under Nicky Haslam and founded her own practice in 2013. Her work is exuberant, witty, and deeply personal: bold colour, hand-painted surfaces, custom furniture with swooping curves and a slightly cartoonish joy, objects chosen for their idiosyncrasy rather than their prestige. Rooms feel like they were designed by someone who genuinely loves being in them. She has been a World of Interiors regular for years; her book Every Room Should Sing (Rizzoli, 2020) is essential reading. Bathrooms are a particular strength: unexpected tile combinations, hand-painted walls, custom mirrors with personality.

Interior Design · Colour · Custom Furniture · Playful Classicism

Axel Vervoordt

Antwerp & Wijnegem, Belgium  ·  Axel & Boris Vervoordt

Axel Vervoordt is one of the great figures of late 20th and early 21st century interior culture — a dealer, designer, and thinker whose wabi-inspired philosophy has influenced a generation of designers. Working from his extraordinary Kanaal complex (a cluster of converted industrial buildings outside Antwerp), and with his son Boris, he has created interiors for private homes, yachts, and the Venetian palazzo Ca' Sagredo that are among the most photographed and discussed of our time. The palette is stone, raw linen, aged oak, and ancient terracotta; the objects are pre-Columbian artifacts, Japanese tea bowls, and 20th century art chosen for spiritual resonance over decorative effect. World of Interiors has returned to his work repeatedly over four decades.

Interior Design · Wabi · Art Dealing · Belgium · Venice

Axel Vervoordt

"Timeless — beyond trend, beyond style"

Vervoordt's site uses a client-rendered architecture that prevents image hotlinking. His work is documented in several monographs including Wabi Inspirations and Atemporal. His Kanaal complex and Venice apartments are benchmark references.

Luke Edward Hall

London & Athens  ·  Luke Edward Hall Studio

Luke Edward Hall is painter, designer, writer, and decorator simultaneously — one of the few practitioners whose sensibility is so distinctive that you can identify a room as his from a single photograph. His interiors are soaked in Mediterranean classicism and European decadence: hand-painted friezes, trompe-l'œil panels, Pompeian reds and deep greens, mythological references, antique busts, draped fabrics. His own home in Athens — documented extensively in World of Interiors — is a masterclass in personal, layered, maximalist decoration with genuine scholarly depth. He writes the Column column for House & Garden and has published two books. His commissions for private residences in London, New York, and Greece show the same painterly intelligence applied at scale.

Interior Decoration · Painting · Classicism · Athens · Maximalism

Luke Edward Hall

"Mediterranean classicism — scholarly and sensual"

Hall's full portfolio of residential projects — Athens apartment, London houses, New York commissions — is on his site. His World of Interiors features and House & Garden columns are essential companion reading.
